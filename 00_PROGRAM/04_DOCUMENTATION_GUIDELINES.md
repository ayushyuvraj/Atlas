# Documentation Guidelines

## Purpose

Define how documents should be written so the repository reads like the operating system of a serious enterprise AI product program.

## Writing Standard

Documents should be:

- Clear
- Structured
- Evidence-oriented
- Executive-readable
- Honest about assumptions
- Useful to Product, Design, Engineering, Business, and public/product narrative stakeholders

## Required Sections

Every major artifact should include, where applicable:

1. Purpose
2. Background
3. Inputs
4. Problem Statement
5. Alternatives Considered
6. Evaluation Criteria
7. Recommendation
8. Decision
9. Tradeoffs
10. Risks
11. Dependencies
12. Open Questions
13. Red Team Review
14. Final Approval
15. Related Artifacts

## Evidence Labels

Every important claim should be labeled clearly as one of:

- Prior chat decision
- Working assumption
- Hypothesis
- Illustrative example
- External research
- Validated customer evidence

## Red Team Requirement

No major document should be marked frozen unless it has gone through Red Team Review.

The review should ask:

- What is weak?
- What is missing?
- What could fail?
- What alternatives were rejected?
- What would a skeptical product leader, enterprise buyer, engineer, or reviewer question?
- What would an enterprise customer question?
- What would engineering, design, AI, security, or GTM challenge?

## Tone

The tone should be serious, direct, and product-leadership oriented.

Avoid:

- Inflated claims
- Fake certainty
- Performative documentation
- Generic AI language
- Unsupported market claims

Prefer:

- Specific reasoning
- Clear tradeoffs
- Honest assumptions
- Decision rationale
- Practical next steps

## Document Status Values

Use these statuses consistently:

- Draft
- In Review
- Revision Needed
- Frozen
- Superseded
