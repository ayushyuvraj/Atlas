# Market Sizing

## Document Status

**Layer:** 2 - Opportunity Discovery  
**Version:** v1.0  
**Status:** Frozen with validation caveat  
**Source:** Reconstructed from the prior Flagship Project chat. No external market-sizing research has been added in this version.  
**Purpose:** Define the market-sizing logic for the selected opportunity without overstating unvalidated numbers.

---

## 1. Sizing Philosophy

This project does not yet claim validated TAM, SAM, or SOM numbers.

Instead, Layer 2 defines the market-sizing logic:

> The market is sized by organizational decision complexity, not by industry.

This is consistent with the chosen positioning: the product should be horizontal across industries rather than trapped in BFSI.

---

## 2. Market Definition

The addressable market consists of organizations that:

- Have 5,000+ employees
- Operate across multiple business units or geographies
- Run formal investment, portfolio, transformation, or executive governance forums
- Make recurring multi-million-dollar strategic decisions
- Already use enterprise collaboration, analytics, and planning tools
- Are actively investing in AI, digital transformation, or operating model change

These organizations exist across:

- Technology
- Healthcare
- Financial services
- Insurance
- Manufacturing
- Retail
- Consumer goods
- Telecom
- Energy
- Logistics
- Public sector

---

## 3. TAM / SAM / SOM Logic

| Layer | Definition | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TAM | All large organizations with recurring strategic decision complexity | Long-term Enterprise Decision OS opportunity |
| SAM | Organizations with formal portfolio, investment, transformation, or strategy governance | Practical enterprise software market |
| SOM | Initial customers willing to pilot AI-assisted strategic portfolio decision support | Near-term wedge |

---

## 4. Initial Wedge Market

The first wedge is:

> Strategic Portfolio & Investment Decision Support

This is attractive because it maps to recurring enterprise processes:

- Annual planning
- Quarterly business reviews
- Investment committees
- Transformation governance
- Product portfolio reviews
- AI initiative prioritization
- Capital allocation reviews

The product does not need every enterprise to buy immediately. It needs one recurring executive governance workflow where decision complexity is painful and budget exists.

---

## 5. Buyer Budget Logic

Likely budget owners:

- Chief Strategy Officer
- Chief Information Officer
- Chief Transformation Officer
- Enterprise PMO / Portfolio Office

Budget justification:

- Reduced decision cycle time
- Better strategic alignment
- Lower risk of failed initiatives
- Better capital allocation
- Improved transformation governance
- Reusable institutional decision memory

---

## 6. Why This Market Is Larger Than BFSI

The prior chat explicitly rejected a banking-only product identity.

BFSI may be a strong example or early use case, but the market is broader because strategic portfolio and investment decisions exist in every complex enterprise.

Market segmentation should therefore be based on:

- Complexity
- Governance maturity
- Investment volume
- Cross-functional decision friction
- AI readiness

not industry alone.

---

## 7. Validation Caveat

This document is a strategic sizing framework, not a validated market-sizing report.

External research still needs to be added later for:

- Current enterprise AI market spend
- Decision intelligence / strategy software categories
- Portfolio management software categories
- Enterprise transformation management spend
- Buyer budget benchmarks

This should be captured in the Research Library and revisited during the Validation and Business Strategy layers.
