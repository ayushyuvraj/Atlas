# Metrics and Success Model

## Document Status

**Layer:** 3 - Product Strategy
**Version:** v1.0
**Status:** Frozen
**Source:** Reconstructed from prior Flagship Project chat.
**Purpose:** Define how product success should be measured at the strategic level.

---

## North Star Metric

The North Star Metric is:

> Decision Quality Improvement

This is the long-term product outcome, not necessarily the first purchase message.

---

## Why Decision Quality

Atlas is designed to improve how organizations make strategic decisions.

Therefore the product should not optimize only for:

- Number of chats
- Number of documents uploaded
- Number of summaries generated
- Time spent in the product
- Number of automated tasks

Those may be useful operational metrics, but they do not capture the product's strategic promise.

---

## Supporting Metrics

| Metric | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Decision cycle time | Time from decision initiation to decision commitment |
| Evidence completeness | Coverage of relevant evidence sources before recommendation |
| Assumption coverage | Number and quality of explicit assumptions captured |
| Stakeholder alignment | Degree of agreement or documented disagreement before commitment |
| Scenario coverage | Number and relevance of scenarios considered |
| Confidence calibration | Whether confidence levels later match actual outcomes |
| Decision reuse | Reuse of prior decisions, assumptions, templates, or lessons |
| Outcome tracking | Percentage of committed decisions reviewed after execution |
| Decision traceability | Ability to reconstruct why a decision was made |

---

## Purchase-Language Metrics

Customers may not buy "decision quality" directly. They are more likely to buy concrete improvements such as:

- Faster investment committee preparation
- Better portfolio governance
- Reduced rework in executive decision forums
- Stronger capital allocation confidence
- Earlier identification of strategic risk
- Clearer decision accountability
- More reusable institutional knowledge

These metrics should be used in GTM and ROI materials.

---

## MVP Success Measures

For the first product wedge, MVP success should be measured by whether Atlas can:

1. Reduce time required to prepare a strategic investment review.
2. Make assumptions and tradeoffs more explicit.
3. Produce an executive-ready recommendation.
4. Preserve the rationale in a structured decision record.
5. Create a reusable artifact for future decisions.

---

## Evaluation Caveat

Decision quality is hard to measure directly. It may require proxy metrics until real outcome data exists.

Early measurement should combine:

- Workflow metrics
- User judgment
- Reasoning completeness
- Review quality
- Outcome follow-up

This caveat should remain visible in later validation work.

---

## Decision

Freeze Decision Quality Improvement as the strategic North Star and use concrete governance, cycle time, alignment, and traceability metrics as MVP proxies.

---

## Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| Decision quality is too abstract | Use measurable supporting metrics and workflow-level ROI proxies |
| Metrics become self-reported only | Add system-generated workflow metrics where possible |
| Early MVP cannot prove outcome quality | Label early metrics as proxy evidence and validate with pilots later |

---

## Final Approval

Frozen for Layer 3. Detailed analytics events belong in Layer 7 Product Definition and Layer 9 Technical Architecture.
