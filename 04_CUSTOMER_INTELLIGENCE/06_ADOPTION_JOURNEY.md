# Adoption Journey

## Document Status

**Layer:** 4 - Customer Intelligence
**Version:** v1.0
**Status:** Frozen
**Source:** Reconstructed from prior Flagship Project chat.
**Purpose:** Define how an organization discovers, evaluates, pilots, adopts, and expands Atlas.

---

## Adoption Thesis

Atlas should not ask customers to replace their executive governance process.

Atlas should upgrade the decision preparation, reasoning, and documentation around existing governance forums.

This reduces change-management friction and makes the product easier to introduce.

---

## Trigger Event

The strongest adoption trigger is:

> A major portfolio, investment, or transformation review is approaching, and the existing preparation process is slow, fragmented, and politically difficult.

Typical situation:

- Many initiatives compete for limited resources.
- Functions disagree on priorities.
- Executive materials are scattered across slides, spreadsheets, and dashboards.
- Assumptions are not consistently documented.
- Leadership needs to approve, delay, kill, or fund initiatives.

---

## Adoption Journey

### Stage 1 - Trigger

A strategic portfolio or investment review exposes friction.

The organization experiences slow preparation, conflicting assumptions, or weak decision traceability.

### Stage 2 - Problem Recognition

Strategy, transformation, or portfolio leaders recognize that the existing process is not simply a reporting problem.

It is a decision governance and reasoning problem.

### Stage 3 - Internal Champion Emerges

A Head of Strategy, Portfolio Director, or Transformation Leader looks for a better way to prepare executive decision forums.

### Stage 4 - Executive Sponsor Alignment

The champion frames the problem in executive language:

- Faster strategic alignment
- Better portfolio governance
- Reduced decision risk
- Clearer capital allocation
- Reusable decision memory

### Stage 5 - Pilot

The organization pilots Atlas with one governance forum, such as:

- Investment committee
- Product portfolio review
- Transformation governance review
- AI initiative prioritization review

### Stage 6 - Proof of Value

The pilot is judged by whether Atlas improves:

- Preparation time
- Assumption clarity
- Executive-ready recommendations
- Traceability
- Stakeholder alignment
- Decision record quality

### Stage 7 - Expansion

If successful, Atlas expands to adjacent decision forums:

```text
Investment Committee
        ->
Transformation Governance
        ->
Product Portfolio Review
        ->
Executive Planning
        ->
Enterprise Decision Operating System
```

## Adoption Barriers

| Barrier | Why It Matters | Product / GTM Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Change management | Executives already have decision rituals | Upgrade existing forums rather than replacing them |
| Trust in AI | Leaders may resist opaque recommendations | Make evidence, assumptions, confidence, and alternatives visible |
| Security concerns | Strategic decisions are sensitive | Build credible security and access-control model later |
| Tool fatigue | Enterprises already have too many tools | Position Atlas as a decision layer over existing artifacts |
| Category ambiguity | Customers may not know they need a Decision OS | Sell concrete outcomes before category language |
| Data availability | Reasoning quality depends on context | Support staged pilots with structured inputs and sample datasets |

---

## Expansion Motion

Atlas expands through recurring decision forums, not through generic seat growth alone.

Expansion path:

1. One strategic review
2. One committee
3. Multiple committees
4. One business unit
5. Multiple business units
6. Enterprise decision memory
7. Enterprise Decision Operating System

---

## Decision

Freeze the adoption journey around a land-and-expand motion that starts with one strategic governance forum and expands through adjacent decision forums.
