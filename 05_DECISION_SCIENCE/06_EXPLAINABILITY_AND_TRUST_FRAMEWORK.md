# Explainability and Trust Framework

## Document Status

**Layer:** 5 - Decision Science
**Version:** v1.0
**Status:** Frozen
**Source:** Reconstructed from prior Flagship Project chat.
**Purpose:** Define how Atlas should earn trust in AI-assisted strategic decision-making.

---

## Trust Thesis

Enterprise leaders will not trust strategic recommendations simply because they are generated by AI.

They are more likely to trust recommendations when they can inspect:

- Evidence
- Assumptions
- Tradeoffs
- Alternatives
- Confidence
- Disagreement
- Missing information
- Historical precedent

Trust is therefore a first-class product capability.

---

## Explainability Requirements

Every recommendation should answer:

1. What is being recommended?
2. Why is it being recommended?
3. What evidence supports it?
4. What assumptions does it depend on?
5. What alternatives were considered?
6. What are the tradeoffs?
7. What risks remain?
8. How confident is the system and why?
9. What would change the recommendation?
10. What must humans decide?

---

## Confidence Model

Confidence should not be presented as false mathematical precision.

It should combine:

- Evidence completeness
- Assumption strength
- Scenario robustness
- Stakeholder alignment
- Historical pattern relevance
- Known uncertainty

Example labels:

- High confidence
- Medium confidence
- Low confidence
- Insufficient evidence

Each confidence label should include rationale.

---

## Trust Signals

| Trust Signal | Product Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence links | Users can inspect sources and summaries |
| Assumption map | Users can see what must be true |
| Alternative analysis | Users can see rejected options |
| Disagreement view | Users can see conflicting perspectives |
| Confidence rationale | Users understand uncertainty |
| Human review status | Users know what humans approved or challenged |
| Decision record | Users can reconstruct final rationale |
| Outcome review | Users can compare expected and actual results |

---

## Challengeability

Every recommendation should be challengeable.

Users should be able to ask:

- Show the opposite conclusion.
- Which assumption matters most?
- What evidence is weakest?
- What would change this recommendation?
- Which stakeholder perspective is missing?
- What historical decision is most similar?

---

## Auditability

Strategic decisions may require review later.

Atlas should preserve:

- Recommendation version
- Evidence used
- Assumptions at time of decision
- Human edits
- Approvals
- Decision record
- Outcome review

Detailed implementation belongs in later technical architecture.

---

## Decision

Freeze explainability, challengeability, confidence rationale, and auditability as core trust requirements.
