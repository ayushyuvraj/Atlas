# Demo Script

## Document Status

**Layer:** 13 - Launch

**Purpose:** Provide a concise walkthrough script for the Atlas MVP.

---

## Opening

Atlas is a working MVP for an Enterprise Decision Operating System.

The product focuses on a common enterprise problem: high-stakes strategic investment decisions are often prepared across slides, spreadsheets, meetings, and disconnected notes. The information may exist, but the reasoning behind the decision is rarely structured, challenged, or preserved.

Atlas turns that process into a Decision Workspace.

---

## Step 1 - Dashboard

Start on the dashboard.

The active decision is a Q4 Strategic Investment Committee review. The organization has multiple competing initiatives and limited budget.

The goal is not for AI to decide. The goal is to help the strategy team prepare a better decision for executive review.

---

## Step 2 - Decision Workspace

Open the Decision Workspace.

This is the core product surface. It keeps the decision statement, objective, owner, sponsor, budget, and lifecycle visible.

The workspace is organized around the decision lifecycle, not around chat.

---

## Step 3 - Institutional Reasoning Engine

Run or review the Institutional Reasoning Engine.

Atlas uses structured reasoning modules:

- Context
- Evidence
- Assumptions
- Perspectives
- Scenarios
- Tradeoffs
- Risks
- Recommendation
- Learning

Each module produces inspectable outputs rather than hiding reasoning behind a single answer.

---

## Step 4 - Assumptions and Perspectives

Show assumptions and perspectives.

The important design decision here is that the system does not force false consensus. Finance, strategy, risk, operations, and technology can surface different concerns before a recommendation is formed.

This reflects the product philosophy:

> AI reasons. Humans decide.

---

## Step 5 - Executive Recommendation

Open the Executive Recommendation.

The recommendation includes:

- Recommended option
- Confidence
- Rationale
- Alternatives
- Open questions
- Evidence trace
- Assumptions
- Risks
- Tradeoffs

This makes the recommendation explainable and challengeable.

---

## Step 6 - Decision Record

Commit the decision.

The commitment step is explicit. Atlas does not approve anything autonomously.

Once committed, the product creates a Decision Record with owner, approver, success metrics, accepted risks, review date, and rationale.

---

## Closing

Atlas is not trying to become another dashboard or chatbot.

It is designed around a larger thesis:

Organizations do not only need to remember what they know. They need to remember how they reasoned, what assumptions they accepted, what risks they carried, and what they learned after the decision.

That is the foundation of an Enterprise Decision Operating System.
